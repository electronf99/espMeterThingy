✅ Original Recommended Architecture (Restated)
🔋 1. Use a DC-DC boost converter
Use a module that can output ~100 V DC at a few watts from your 15 V supply.
Examples I previously suggested:

Sunuaryuan GRFxxxxS isolated high‑voltage converter
Or a typical adjustable 100 V boost converter (like your eBay one)

You set the boost converter output to ~100 V and leave it fixed.
This provides the “high voltage rail” needed for the meter.

🔧 2. Do not PWM the 100 V
Instead, the Pico controls current, not the voltage.
The meter responds to current, and moving‑iron movements require smooth DC, not high‑voltage PWM.

⭐ 3. Use a MOSFET + op‑amp constant current driver
This is the heart of the design.
The Pico outputs a low‑voltage analogue control signal:
0–3.3 V (DAC or filtered PWM)
That goes into an op‑amp, which:

compares the control signal to a feedback voltage
adjusts the MOSFET gate
sinks exactly 0–20 mA through the meter
regardless of the 100 V supply or meter resistance

This gives a perfectly linear meter response.

🎯 Summary
The boost converter supplies the ~100 V.
The MOSFET/op‑amp controls the current through the meter.
The Pico sets the current level via a 0–3.3 V analog control signal.
This is the correct, safe way to control an old‑style HV analog meter.
